## Azure first

- The **Microsoft Defender portal** (`security.microsoft.com`) is the website where the SOC works. Its **Settings** page holds one block of settings per product. This lesson lives in **Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR**.
- An **incident** is a case file that groups related **alerts** (single suspicious signals) into one attack story. A **response action** is something done to a device, mailbox or user — isolate a laptop, delete an email — either by an analyst or by automation.
- **Threat analytics** is a library of reports written by Microsoft researchers about active attackers and campaigns.
- A **device group** is a named set of laptops/servers in Defender for Endpoint. It is used to limit who sees what. Notification rules can be scoped to device groups.
- **Licence:** this needs a Microsoft 365 tenant with Defender XDR products. With an Azure subscription only, treat this page and its screenshots as the lab.

New to these? Read the Foundations pages linked above.

## In one sentence

Email notification rules in **Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR > Email notifications** send an email to chosen people when a new incident appears, when a response action runs, or when a threat analytics report is published or updated.

## Why it exists

Nobody watches the incident queue 24 hours a day. A smoke detector is useless if nobody hears it. Email notifications are the doorbell: the on-call analyst's phone buzzes when something important lands, without them sitting in the portal.

Each of the three tabs answers a different "tell me when…" question:

- **Incidents** — "tell me when a new attack story starts (or grows)".
- **Actions** — "tell me when something was *done*, automatically or by a colleague, and whether it worked".
- **Threat analytics** — "tell me when Microsoft publishes or updates intelligence about a threat I care about".

## What actually matters

- **Three tabs, three triggers.** **Incidents**, **Actions**, **Threat analytics**. Microsoft's training unit lists only two (Incidents and Threat Analytics). The live product and docs have **three** — the product wins.
- **Incident rules filter on alerts.** You choose **alert severity**, the **service sources / detection sources** (you can set a severity per source — for example Medium and High for EDR, everything for Defender Experts) and the **device group scope**. Two toggles are worth knowing: **Send only one notification per incident** and **Include tenant-specific portal link**. **Include organization name** is the third.
- **Action rules filter on what was done.** **Action source** = manual, automated, or both. Automated covers **automatic attack disruption** and **automated investigation and response (AIR)**. Then pick the **actions**, the **device groups scope** (**All device groups** or **Selected device groups**) and the **Action status** (completed and/or failed). Response-action emails ==!do not cover custom detections that contain response actions==.
- **Threat analytics rules filter on reports.** All new/updated reports, or only reports of a **type** or with a **tag**.
- **Who can configure.** The **Manage security settings** permission. With basic permissions, **Security Administrator** or higher. With RBAC (role-based access control), you can only create, edit, delete — and receive — notifications for **device groups you are allowed to manage**.
- **Recipients and lifecycle.** Add one or more addresses; use **Send test email**. New recipients only get notifications raised *after* they are added. ==!Deleting a rule is permanent== — there is no undo.

## Where it lives

All in the **Microsoft Defender portal**. Path: **Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR > General > Email notifications**, then a tab.

| Tab | Button | Wizard pages | Finish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incidents | **Add incident notification rule** | Basics > Notification settings > Recipients > Review rule | **Create rule** |
| Actions | **Add notification rule** | Basics > Notification settings > Recipients > Review rule | **Submit** |
| Threat analytics | **+ Create a notification rule** | name/description > reports > recipients > review | **Create rule**, then **Done** |

There is no PowerShell cmdlet for these rules; the exam tests the portal path.

The portal-overview article frames where these rules sit. **Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR** is the page that holds *"Email notifications for incidents, response actions, and threat analytics reports"*, next to permissions and roles, streaming API, asset rule management, alert tuning and critical asset management. Do not confuse email rules with the **bell icon** in the portal's top bar. The bell shows in-portal notifications (Success, Ongoing, Information, Warning, Error) to whoever is signed in. Dismissing one hides it; it does not delete it. Nobody gets an email from the bell.

## Exam traps

- "Email me when a **new alert** fires on a device in the Finance group" → ==!not this page==. That is ==+Settings > Endpoints > Email notifications (Alerts tab)== — see D1.1.2. This page notifies on **incidents**, **actions** and **reports**.
- "Email me when **automatic attack disruption** contains a user" → ==+Actions tab, Action source = Automated==. Not the Incidents tab.
- "Only one email per incident, not one per new alert" → ==+Send only one notification per incident==.
- "Recipient must click straight into the right tenant" (an MSSP with many tenants) → ==+Include tenant-specific portal link==.
- An analyst scoped to one device group cannot create a rule for **all** device groups. RBAC scope limits both managing *and* receiving.
- "Make sure the on-call analyst gets an **email**" → ==+an email notification rule==. ==!The bell icon== only shows in-portal messages to signed-in users.
- Threat analytics notifications are **email rules**, ==!not== a Sentinel automation rule or a Logic App.
