## Azure first

- **The Defender portal** (`security.microsoft.com`) shows threat analytics. It is not in the Azure portal.
- **Threat intelligence (TI)** is knowledge about attackers: who they are, how they work, and the **indicators of compromise (IOCs)** they leave — file hashes, IP addresses, domains.
- **MITRE ATT&CK** is a public catalogue of attacker techniques (for example "Credential access"). Reports map each threat to it.
- **Licences decide what you see.** A Defender product licence (not Defender for Endpoint P1) opens every tab. A **Microsoft Sentinel-only** tenant sees only some tabs (preview). Your Azure-subscription lab is Sentinel-only.
- **Secure Score** is Microsoft's posture-points meter; the Recommended actions tab tracks only items Secure Score tracks.

New to these? Read the Foundations pages linked above.

## In one sentence

Threat analytics is a library of Microsoft-written reports on current attackers, campaigns, techniques and vulnerabilities, where each report is overlaid with *your* data — which incidents, assets and unpatched devices in your tenant relate to that threat.

## Why it exists

A news article says "a ransomware gang is exploiting a new VPN bug". The questions that follow are always the same. *Are we hit? What did it touch? Are we exposed? What do we fix first?*

Think of a weather service that does not just say "storm coming" but sends a map with **your** house on it: the windows already broken, the ones still open, and a list of what to shut. The analyst report is the forecast. The tabs around it are your house.

## What actually matters

### 1. The dashboard

Open threat analytics and you land on a dashboard (`security.microsoft.com/threatanalytics3`) with three lists:

| Section | Ranks threats by |
|---|---|
| **Latest threats** | Most recently published or updated reports, with active / resolved alert counts |
| **High-impact threats** | Most active and resolved alerts in **your** organization |
| **Highest exposure threats** | Your **exposure**: severity of the related vulnerabilities × how many of your devices could be exploited |

Filter the list by **Threat tags** (Ransomware, Phishing, Activity group, Vulnerability) or by **Category** (report type). Columns on the main list include **Misconfigured devices** and **Vulnerable devices**; they show **Not available** when researchers linked no recommendations or vulnerabilities.

### 2. The report tabs

| Tab | Gives you |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | Preview of the analyst report; aliases, origin, related intelligence, targets, MITRE techniques; charts for related incidents, alerts over time, impacted assets, recommended actions, endpoints exposure |
| **Analyst report** | The full expert write-up — attack chain, detections, **hunting queries**, recommendations |
| **Related incidents** | Every incident in your tenant tied to this threat. Assign them or manage their alerts from here |
| **Impacted assets** | Devices, users, mailboxes, apps and cloud resources with active or resolved alerts for this threat |
| **Endpoints exposure** | Your **exposure level** and the deployment status of security updates (data from Defender Vulnerability Management) |
| **Recommended actions** | Mitigations and their status — for example cloud-delivered protection, PUA protection, real-time protection |
| **Indicators** (preview) | IOCs behind the threat, updated in real time; expired IOCs stay listed for looking back. ==!Only verified tenants== see them |

Two limits to remember: **Recommended actions** lists **only Secure Score-tracked** items (the analyst report has more), and recommended actions do not guarantee resilience.

### 3. The analyst report — report types and sections

**Report types (Category filter):** **Activity** (a campaign), **Actor** (a threat actor profile), **Core threat** (a narrative across several profiles), **Technique**, **Tool**, **Vulnerability** (a CVE), and **OSINT** (preview — curated public-source articles).

**Sections inside:** Executive summary · Overview · **Detections / Hunting queries** · MITRE ATT&CK techniques observed · Recommendations · References · Change log.

The hunting queries are vetted by Microsoft analysts. You run them in **advanced hunting** and can turn them into **custom detection rules** (D1.4.1). Reports may also include **Microsoft Sentinel** hunting queries. A typical one sweeps the report's indicators:

```kql
// Defender — sweep IOCs copied from a report's Indicators tab
let iocIPs = dynamic(["<ip-1>", "<ip-2>"]);
let iocHashes = dynamic(["<sha256-1>"]);
DeviceNetworkEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(30d)
| where RemoteIP in (iocIPs)
| project Timestamp, DeviceName, RemoteIP, RemotePort, InitiatingProcessFileName
| union (
    DeviceFileEvents
    | where Timestamp > ago(30d)
    | where SHA256 in (iocHashes)
    | project Timestamp, DeviceName, FileName, SHA256 )
```

```kql
// Sentinel (runs in the Log Analytics demo) — the same idea against Azure sign-ins
let iocIPs = dynamic(["<ip-1>", "<ip-2>"]);
SigninLogs
| where TimeGenerated > ago(30d)
| where IPAddress in (iocIPs)
| project TimeGenerated, UserPrincipalName, IPAddress, AppDisplayName, ResultType
```

### 4. Who sees what

- **Licence:** at least one Microsoft Defender product. ==!Defender for Endpoint P1 does not grant threat analytics.== Every report is visible with one product, but each product's **incidents, assets, exposure and actions** need that product.
- **Sentinel-only (preview):** **Overview**, **Analyst report** and **Indicators** only. Related incidents, impacted assets, endpoints exposure and recommended actions stay locked.
- **Permissions (unified RBAC):** **Security data basics (read)** for reports, related incidents/alerts and impacted assets; **Vulnerability management (read)** and **Exposure Management (read)** for exposure data and recommended actions.

### 5. Staying current

- **Threat Intelligence Briefing Agent** (Security Copilot) — appears as a banner on the threat analytics page and writes a tailored briefing from recent actor activity and your vulnerability exposure. Needs Security Copilot plus the *Microsoft Threat Intelligence* and *Microsoft Threat Intelligence agents* plugins (*Defender External Attack Surface Management* is optional). The account needs Defender Vulnerability Management data access, **Security Reader** to read results and **Security Admin** to onboard and configure. **Run agent** refreshes; **View full brief** opens a panel you can copy or download as markdown. Past briefings are under **Activity** in the Security Copilot portal. Settings: **Manage agent**, or **System > Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR > Threat Intelligence Briefing Agent**.
- **Custom detections can link to a report** — their incidents then appear under that report's **Related incidents**.
- **Email notifications** for report updates (a notification rule type — see D1.1.1).
- **Download as PDF** exports the threat context, analyst report and related intelligence.

## Exam traps

- ==!"Threat analytics is a Sentinel feature in the Azure portal."== ==+It is a Defender portal feature== (Sentinel-only tenants get a partial view there).
- ==!"Recommended actions lists every mitigation."== ==+Only Secure Score-tracked ones==; read the analyst report for the rest.
- **High-impact vs Highest exposure:** *alerts you already have* vs *vulnerable devices you could have*. Classic confusable.
- **Impacted assets** counts assets with **at least one active alert** for the threat — not every device running the vulnerable software (that is Endpoints exposure).
- **Defender for Endpoint P1** does not unlock threat analytics.
- **Indicators** need a **verified tenant**.
- **Hunting queries live inside the analyst report** — run them in advanced hunting, or build a custom detection from them.
