## Azure first

- A **cloud app** (SaaS app) is software you use in a browser and never install: Microsoft 365, Salesforce, Dropbox, Google Workspace, and thousands of others your users try without asking IT.
- **Defender for Cloud Apps (MDA)** sits between users and those apps. It reads their activity through **app connectors** (APIs), reads your **firewall/proxy logs** to spot apps nobody approved, and can sit inline in a browser session through **Conditional Access App Control**.
- An **OAuth app** is a third-party app a user has allowed to act on their behalf ("This app wants to read your mailbox"). The permission it got is a door an attacker can use.
- MDA is now part of the **Defender portal** (`security.microsoft.com`) under **Cloud apps**. Its alerts land in the shared **Incidents & alerts** queue. Its user actions reach into **Microsoft Entra ID** (suspend, sign out, mark compromised).

New to these? Read the Foundations pages linked above.

> **Licence-blocked for an Azure-only learner.** MDA needs a Defender for Cloud Apps licence (in Microsoft 365 E5 / E5 Security) and connected SaaS apps. The training module's walkthrough is a video. Use the screenshots below as the lab.

## In one sentence

Defender for Cloud Apps finds risky **cloud app use** (unapproved apps, odd sign-ins, mass downloads, over-permissioned OAuth apps); you investigate its **alerts** with the **activity log**, the **user and IP insights** and the **OAuth app / app governance** pages, then remediate with **governance actions** — suspend the user, force re-sign-in, confirm compromised, unsanction or block the app, revoke the app.

## Why it exists

Your firewall sees `dropbox.com` traffic. It does not see that Bob uploaded 4,000 files from an IP in a country he has never visited, or that a "Calendar Helper" app has read access to every mailbox. Cloud apps sit outside your network, so the old perimeter tools are blind to what happens *inside* them.

A **cloud access security broker (CASB)** fixes that. Gartner's analogy fits: a CASB is to cloud services what a firewall is to the corporate network. Think of a building's front desk that also keeps the visitor book for every *other* building your staff visit: who went where, what they carried out, and which outside contractors hold copies of your keys.

## What actually matters

**1. The four jobs of the CASB.** The module frames MDA as four elements: **discover and control Shadow IT**, **protect sensitive information**, **protect against cyberthreats and anomalies**, and **assess compliance** of cloud apps. SC-200 cares most about the first and third: unapproved apps and suspicious behaviour.

**2. Where the evidence comes from.**

| Source | Feeds | Gives you |
|---|---|---|
| **App connectors** (API) | **Activity log** | Who did what inside connected apps (Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Salesforce, Box…) |
| **Discovery logs** (firewall/proxy upload, or Defender for Endpoint) | **Cloud discovery** | Which apps are used, by whom, how much |
| **Proxy logs** (Conditional Access App Control) | Session monitoring | Real-time control inside a browser session |

**3. Anomaly detection alerts.** Built-in, on by default, behaviour-based (**UEBA** = user and entity behaviour analytics: learn "normal", alert on "different"). MDA has a **seven-day** learning period in which not all anomaly alerts fire (new locations never alert in that week); after that each session is compared with the past month. Over **30 risk indicators** roll up into eight risk factors: risky IP, login failures, admin activity, inactive accounts, location, **impossible travel**, device and user agent, and activity rate. Detections you should recognise by name: **Impossible travel**, **Activity from infrequent country**, **Malware detection**, **Unusual file download (by user)**, **Unusual administrative activity (by user)**, **Unusual addition of credentials to an OAuth app**. There is also a **Cloud Discovery anomaly detection** policy that alerts on unusual jumps in an app's usage (uploads, downloads, transactions, users).

==!Sources disagree here.== The training module (unit 7) still lists **Ransomware activity**, **Activity from suspicious IP addresses** and **Suspicious inbox forwarding** as "the most popular" anomaly policies. The product docs say that since **June 2025** MDA is moving anomaly policies to a **dynamic threat detection model**, and those legacy policies — plus *Suspicious inbox manipulation rules*, *Suspicious email deletion activity*, *Activity from anonymous IP addresses*, *Unusual ISP for an OAuth app*, *Suspicious file access activity (by user)* and *Activity performed by terminated user* — are **disabled** in Policy management. Their detection continues in the new model; *Activity from anonymous IP address* became *Activity from a TOR IP address* and *Anonymous proxy activity*. **The product docs win:** expect the names, but do not expect to tune those legacy policies. You can tune each remaining policy's **sensitivity** and **scope** (include/exclude users and groups):

| Sensitivity | Suppression applied |
|---|---|
| Low | System, Tenant and User |
| Medium | System and User |
| High | System only |

*System* = built-in suppressions; *Tenant* = activity common in your organisation (e.g. an ISP already alerted on); *User* = activity common for that user (e.g. their usual location). For impossible travel, if **both** IPs are tagged safe (e.g. **corporate**) and sensitivity is not **High**, no alert fires.

**4. Investigating an alert.** Every MDA alert is classified after investigation:

| Class | Meaning | Typical action |
|---|---|---|
| **TP** (true positive) | Confirmed malicious | **Suspend user**, **mark user as compromised**, **reset password**; review all their activity |
| **B-TP** (benign true positive) | Real but authorised (pen test, a known traveller) | **Dismiss**; exclude the user or tag the IP |
| **FP** (false positive) | Not malicious | Dismiss; tune the policy |

The **Activity log** (Cloud apps > Activity log) is the main tool. Filter it (basic or advanced filters), then open an activity. The **activity drawer** has a **User** tab — open alerts, policy matches, activities, countries, ISPs and IP addresses over the **last 30 days** — and an **IP address** tab where you can tag the IP as **risky**, **VPN** or **corporate**. **New policy from search** turns a useful filter into an activity policy. Compare the user's OS, browser and IP/location with their normal devices. Then check **Files** (public or external shares) and **Identities** (inactive accounts, leavers who still have access).

**5. Governance actions — the remediation verbs.**

| On | Action | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| User | **Suspend user** | No access, no sign-in; signed-in users are locked out. ==!Reverted if on-prem AD sync overrides it== |
| User | **Require user to sign in again** | Revokes refresh tokens and session cookies |
| User | **Confirm user compromised** | Sets the user's **Entra ID risk to High** — Entra risk-based Conditional Access policies then enforce |
| User | Revoke admin privileges / Unsuspend | Google Workspace / multiple apps |
| File | Remove public or external sharing, make private, remove collaborator | Change sharing |
| File | Put in **user** or **admin quarantine**, **trash**, apply/remove sensitivity label | Contain the file |
| App | **Sanctioned / Unsanctioned** | Unsanctioned apps are **blocked** if MDE integration is on; otherwise generate a **block script** for your firewall |
| OAuth app | **Ban** / approve / **revoke** / notify user | Revoke and notify: Google Workspace and Salesforce only. **Ban** in Google/Salesforce revokes and blocks future consent; ==!in Microsoft 365, Ban blocks new access but does not revoke existing permissions== |

Every manual or automatic action is recorded in the **Governance log**. Malware governance actions need **Security Operator**, **Security Administrator** or **Cloud App Security Administrator**. ==!File policies retire on 6 January 2027== — Microsoft says move file protection to Purview DLP or auto-labelling.

**6. Discovered apps (Shadow IT).** **Cloud apps > Cloud discovery** shows a dashboard: usage overview, top categories, **Discovered apps**, top users and IPs, **App headquarters map**, and **App risk overview**. Each app gets a **risk score from 1 to 10** — a weighted average of four categories: **General**, **Security**, **Compliance** and **Legal**. ==!Numbers differ by source:== the training module says 80+ factors over 16,000+ apps; the current product docs say **more than 90 risk factors** over **more than 31,000 apps** in the **Cloud app catalog**. Docs win. You can override an app's score and request a score update. Marking an app **Unsanctioned** does not by itself block it — blocking needs **Defender for Endpoint** (with Cloud Protection, Network Protection, and the Browser Protection add-on for non-Microsoft browsers), a supported secure web gateway (Zscaler, iboss, Corrata, Menlo, Open Systems), or an exported **block script**. Some Microsoft security services are **non-blockable**.

**7. OAuth apps and app governance.** App governance raises alerts about malicious or over-permissioned OAuth apps into the normal Defender **Alerts** and **Incidents** pages. The alert's **Alert story** says **What happened** and gives **Recommended actions**. Mark it **In progress**, open the app entity (**View app details**), then **Resolved**. To hunt, filter **OAuth apps** by **permission level: high** and **community use: not common**, or use the saved query **Apps authorized by external users**. Attack disruption can also act here automatically (**OAuth app compromise**, D1.1.8).

**8. Conditional Access App Control.** Prevention, not investigation, but you will be asked about it. An Entra Conditional Access policy with **Session > Use Conditional Access App Control** routes the session through MDA. **Access policies** decide who gets in; **session policies** block download, cut/copy/print, upload of unlabelled files, or messages with sensitive content, in real time.

## Where it lives

All in the **Defender portal** (`security.microsoft.com`).

| Task | Path |
|---|---|
| Alerts and incidents | **Incidents & alerts > Alerts / Incidents** |
| Activity log | **Cloud apps > Activity log** |
| Shadow IT | **Cloud apps > Cloud discovery** (Dashboard, Discovered apps) |
| Sanction / unsanction | **Cloud apps > Cloud app catalog** or **Cloud discovery > Discovered apps** > row menu |
| Policies and templates | **Cloud apps > Policies > Policy management / Policy templates** |
| OAuth apps / app governance | **Cloud apps > OAuth apps** (or **App governance** when enabled) |
| Governance log | **Cloud apps > Governance log** |
| App connectors | **Settings > Cloud Apps > Connected apps > App connectors** |
| Users | **Identities** (user page: Suspend, Remove collaborations, Entra account settings) |

Advanced hunting: `CloudAppEvents` holds the activity data; `BehaviorInfo` / `BehaviorEntities` hold MDA behaviours.

## Exam traps

- ==!Unsanctioned means blocked.== ==+Unsanctioned is a tag. Blocking needs MDE integration, a supported gateway, or a block script.==
- ==!Confirm user compromised just tags the user in MDA.== ==+It sets the user's Entra ID risk to High, so Entra risk-based Conditional Access policies enforce.==
- **Sign the user out of everything now** → ==+Require user to sign in again== (revokes refresh tokens and session cookies). **Stop them signing in at all** → ==+Suspend user==.
- **Pen-tester triggered "Activity from suspicious IP"** → ==@B-TP== → dismiss and exclude.
- ==!Anomaly policies alert from day one.== ==+They have a seven-day learning period first.==
- ==!You still tune "Ransomware activity" and "Suspicious inbox forwarding" policies.== ==+Since June 2025 those legacy policies are disabled; detection moved to the dynamic threat detection model.==
- **Mark app as approved** on the OAuth page ==!does not change anything for users== — it is a review marker only.
- **Higher sensitivity = fewer suppressions** (High suppresses System only). ==!Not the other way round.==
- **Stop downloads of sensitive files on unmanaged devices in real time** → ==+session policy via Conditional Access App Control==, not an activity policy.
- **OAuth app policies and the malware detection policy** are built-in; ==!you cannot create new ones of those types.==
- ==!Suspend user always sticks.== ==+If on-prem AD sync is authoritative, it reverts the suspension.==
