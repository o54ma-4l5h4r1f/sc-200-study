## Azure first

- **Microsoft Entra ID** holds your users and **groups**. Defender for Endpoint never lists people one by one — it grants access to **Entra user groups**.
- **Entra roles** such as **Security Administrator** and **Security Reader** are tenant-wide job titles. Before any finer setup, they decide who sees the Defender portal at all.
- **RBAC (role-based access control)** means "a role is a bundle of permissions; you give the role to a group". The Defender portal has had two RBAC systems: the old **Defender for Endpoint roles** and the newer **Defender unified RBAC**.
- A **device tag** is a label you stick on a device (for example `Finance-Servers`). Device groups often match on tags.

New to these? Read the Foundations pages linked above.

## In one sentence

A **device group** is a named slice of your onboarded devices (matched by name, domain, tag or OS) that carries two settings — **who may see and act on those devices** and **how much automatic remediation happens on them** — and **roles** decide *what* those people may do.

## Why it exists

A global company might have a London SOC, a Tokyo SOC and a small team that looks after the payment servers. The London analysts should see London laptops, not the payment servers. The payment servers should never be auto-quarantined without a human saying yes. Ordinary laptops should be fixed automatically.

Think of a hotel. **Roles** are job descriptions: cleaner, receptionist, manager. **Device groups** are the floors. A key card gives a *job* on certain *floors*. And each floor has its own rule for the fire system: sprinklers fire at once on the guest floors, but the server room asks before flooding.

## What actually matters

**1. What a device group does — four jobs.**

- Limits who sees alerts and data for those devices (to the Entra user groups you pick).
- Sets a different **auto-remediation** level for each set of devices.
- Applies that remediation level during automated investigations and attack disruption (D1.1.7, D1.1.8).
- Lets analysts filter the **Devices** list with the **Group** filter.

Device groups exist in Defender for Endpoint **Plan 1 and Plan 2**. Limit: **2,000** device groups per tenant.

**2. Matching and ranking — the most tested part.**

- Conditions: **device name**, **domain**, **tags**, **OS platform**.
- A device must match **all** the conditions you set (AND across different properties).
- Several rows of the **same** property are **OR** — "tag is `London` OR tag is `Paris`". Up to **10 rows** per property.
- ==@A device joins exactly one group: the highest-ranked group it matches.== **Rank 1 is the highest.** You promote or demote ranks after creation.
- A device that matches nothing lands in ==@Ungrouped devices (default)==. You **cannot delete or re-rank** it, but you **can** set its remediation level and user access.
- **Show preview** on the wizard displays up to **10** matching devices.
- ==!A device group with no Entra user groups assigned is visible to everyone== who has portal access.
- Changes take minutes, sometimes hours, to apply. Deleting a group also removes it from email notification rules (and deletes a rule if it was the only group).

**3. Automation (remediation) levels.** Set on each device group.

| Level (docs name) | Wizard label | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| ==+Full - remediate threats automatically== | Full remediation | Malicious items fixed automatically; see them on the Action center **History** tab. **Recommended.** |
| Semi - require approval for all folders | Semi - Approval required for all folders | Every file remediation waits on the **Pending** tab. |
| Semi - require approval for core folders remediation | Semi - Approval required for system folders | Approval only for OS folders such as `\windows\*`; other folders fixed automatically. |
| Semi - require approval for non-temp folders remediation | Semi - Approval required for non-temporary folders | Temp/download folders fixed automatically; everything else waits. |
| No automated response | No automated response | Automated investigation does not run; no AIR actions. Not recommended. |

Defaults: tenants created **on or after 16 August 2020** with no device groups get **Full**. Older tenants get **Semi - require approval for all folders**. Device groups you already defined are never changed by new defaults. Pending actions time out after **7 days** (treated as rejected). In Defender for Business everything is Full and not configurable.

The same levels appear in **Settings > Endpoints > General > Auto remediation** (a quick list of groups and their level) and in **Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR > Automated response > Devices** (attack disruption). One setting, three doors.

**4. Permissions model A — classic Defender for Endpoint roles.**

- Before you turn roles on: Entra **Security Administrator** (and Global Administrator) get **full access**; Entra **Security Reader** gets **read-only**.
- ==!Turning on roles removes access from read-only users (such as Security Reader) until they are put in a role.== ==!You cannot switch back afterwards.==
- Admins are put into the built-in **Defender for Endpoint Administrator** role, which sees **all devices regardless of device group**.
- Create roles in **Settings** > **Endpoints** > **Permissions** > **Roles** > **Add role**, then assign to an **Entra security group**. Then give that group access on the device group's **User access** page — you can only pick Entra groups already assigned to a role.

Classic role permissions:

| Permission | Lets you |
|---|---|
| View data — Security operations / Defender Vulnerability Management | See data in the portal |
| ==@Active remediation actions== — Security operations | Take response actions, **approve or dismiss pending actions**, manage automation allow/block lists and indicators |
| Active remediation actions — DVM exception / remediation / application handling | Vulnerability-management workflows |
| Security baselines | Manage baseline assessment profiles |
| Alerts investigation | Manage alerts, start investigations, run scans, collect investigation packages, manage device tags, download PE files |
| ==@Manage portal system settings== | Storage, SIEM, API, advanced settings, automated file uploads, **roles and device groups**. **Only in the Administrator role.** |
| Manage security settings in Security Center | Alert suppression, **automation folder exclusions**, onboard/offboard, email notifications, evaluation lab, indicators |
| Live response — Basic / Advanced | Basic: start a session, read-only commands, download a file. Advanced: upload files, run scripts from the library. |

The tier model Microsoft suggests: **Tier 1** local team triages, **Tier 2** regional team remediates, **Tier 3** global team does everything.

**5. Permissions model B — Defender unified RBAC (now the default).**

- ==@Since 16 February 2025, new Defender for Endpoint customers get only unified RBAC.== Existing customers keep their old roles until they migrate.
- Built in **System** > **Permissions** > **Microsoft Defender XDR** > **Roles** > **Create custom role**.
- Permission groups: **Security operations**, **Security posture**, **Authorization and settings**, **Data operations** (preview).
- Each role has **assignments**: which users or groups, and which **data sources** (Defender for Endpoint, Defender for Office 365, Defender for Identity, Sentinel workspaces …). One role can carry several assignments.
- ==!Unified RBAC roles do not scope devices by themselves.== ==+Device groups still decide which devices a user sees==; set user access on the device group.
- A workload only obeys unified RBAC after you **activate** it (Roles > **Workload settings** or **Activate workloads**, or **Settings** > **Microsoft Defender XDR** > **Permissions and roles**). Deactivating reverts to the old model. You can **import** existing MDE roles first.
- To manage roles you need Entra **Security Administrator** or a custom role with the **Authorization** permission. Entra roles keep working after activation.

Unified RBAC permissions that map to MDE work:

| Unified RBAC permission | Lets you |
|---|---|
| Security operations / Security data basics (read) | View incidents, alerts, investigations, hunting, devices |
| Security operations / Alerts (manage) | Manage alerts, start investigations, run scans, collect packages, manage device tags |
| ==@Security operations / Response (manage)== | Response actions, **approve or dismiss pending remediation** |
| Basic live response / Advanced live response (manage) | Live response sessions; advanced = upload and run scripts |
| File collection (manage) | Collect or download files |
| Authorization and settings / Core security settings (manage) | Manage core security settings (used for endpoint security policies on D1.1.6 and attack-disruption exclusions on D1.1.8) |
| Authorization and settings / Authorization | Create and manage roles |

## Where it lives

| Task | Portal | Path |
|---|---|---|
| Create / rank device groups | Defender portal | **System** > **Settings** > **Endpoints** > **Permissions** > **Device groups** > **Add device group** (`security.microsoft.com/securitysettings/endpoints/machine_groups`) |
| Quick remediation-level list | Defender portal | **System** > **Settings** > **Endpoints** > **General** > **Auto remediation** |
| Classic MDE roles | Defender portal | **System** > **Settings** > **Endpoints** > **Permissions** > **Roles** |
| Unified RBAC roles | Defender portal | **System** > **Permissions** > **Microsoft Defender XDR** > **Roles** (`security.microsoft.com/mtp_roles`) |
| Activate unified RBAC | Defender portal | Roles > **Workload settings**, or **System** > **Settings** > **Microsoft Defender XDR** > **Permissions and roles** |
| Create the Entra groups | Entra admin center | **Groups** > **All groups** > **New group** |

Wizard pages for a device group: **General** (name, remediation level, description) → **Devices** (matching rule) → **Preview devices** → **User access** → **Submit**.

## Exam traps

- ==!A device in two matching groups belongs to both.== ==+It belongs only to the highest-ranked one== (rank 1 is highest).
- ==!You can delete or re-rank "Ungrouped devices".== You can only change its remediation level and user access.
- ==!A new device group with no user groups is hidden.== It is **visible to all** portal users.
- ==!Security Reader keeps read access after turning on MDE roles.== They **lose** access until assigned a role, and the change is **irreversible**.
- "Analyst must approve pending actions on Finance devices only" → role with **Active remediation actions** (classic) or **Response (manage)** (unified), **and** user access on the **Finance** device group.
- "Who can create device groups and roles?" → **Manage portal system settings** (only the Administrator role) or Entra **Security Administrator**.
- ==!Unified RBAC data-source scoping replaces device groups.== Device groups still scope MDE devices.
- ==!Semi-automation stops attack disruption.== Only **No automated response** excludes a group from disruption.
- Default level question → tenant created **before 16 August 2020** = Semi (all folders); **on/after** = Full.
- Conditions on different properties are **AND**; rows of the same property are **OR**.

**Two Microsoft sources disagree.** (1) The automation-level article says **core folders**; the device-group wizard says **system folders**; training unit 4 calls the strictest level *"approval for any remediation"*. Same settings — the wizard wording is what you see in the product. (2) Training unit 6 still shows a **Turn on roles** button, uses the old name *Threat and vulnerability management*, puts *download a file* under **Advanced** live response, and lists a *Manage endpoint security settings in Microsoft Intune* permission. The current docs say *Defender Vulnerability Management*, put *download a file* under **Basic**, and new tenants since 16 February 2025 do not get these classic roles at all. Trust the current docs.

## Skip

- **Skip:** the Defender Vulnerability Management permissions (exception handling, remediation handling, application handling, security baselines). They are posture-management work, not SOC operations.
- **Skip:** unified RBAC for Sentinel workspaces, the data lake, GDAP remote tenant groups and multi-tenant management. Admin rollout, not this bullet.
- **Skip:** the Defender for Cloud and Defender for Identity scoping options inside unified RBAC.
- **Skip:** the full permission-mapping table between old and new models. Know the three that matter: Active remediation actions ↔ Response (manage); Alerts investigation ↔ Alerts (manage); live response basic/advanced ↔ the two live response permissions.
- **Skip:** the "Is your SOC running flat with RBAC" blog link and the tier-model essay. One line is enough: Tier 1 triage, Tier 2 remediate, Tier 3 everything.
- **Skip:** training unit 4's step to turn on **Automatically resolve alerts** in Advanced features — that belongs to D1.1.3/D1.1.7.
- **Skip:** Defender for Business device groups. They work differently and are not on the exam.
