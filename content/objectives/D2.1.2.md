## Azure first

- **Microsoft Purview** is Microsoft's data-protection and compliance family. It has its own portal, `purview.microsoft.com`. Security analysts visit it less often than the Defender portal, but its alerts end up in front of them.
- **Data loss prevention (DLP)** is a set of rules that watch sensitive data (card numbers, passport numbers, labelled files) in email, SharePoint, OneDrive, Teams and devices. A rule can warn, block and raise an **alert**.
- **Insider Risk Management (IRM)** watches *people* rather than single actions. It scores a user's behaviour over time (a resignation date, then mass downloads, then uploads to personal cloud) and raises an alert when the score passes a threshold.
- Both send their alerts to the **Defender portal** too, where they can join incidents with endpoint, email and identity alerts. Access needs **roles in both** products and a Microsoft 365 **E5-class** compliance licence.

New to these? Read the Foundations pages linked above.

> **Licence-blocked for an Azure-only learner.** DLP and IRM need Microsoft 365 E5 / E5 Compliance (or equivalent) and real Microsoft 365 workloads. The training modules ship interactive simulations instead — use them, and treat every screenshot below as the lab.

## In one sentence

Purview raises **DLP alerts** (one action broke a data rule) and **insider risk alerts** (one person's behaviour crossed a risk score); you triage them in the **Purview portal** or in the **Defender portal incident queue**, investigate the matched content and the user's activity, then remediate the file, the user or the policy — and for insider risk, escalate to a **case**.

## Why it exists

Not every breach is an outsider. A departing employee copies the customer list to a USB stick. A finance clerk emails a spreadsheet of card numbers to the wrong address. Neither is malware, so Defender for Endpoint stays quiet.

Think of a museum. **DLP** is the alarm on a single display case: someone lifted *this* object. **Insider Risk** is the guard who notices one visitor has walked past the same case six times this week, photographed the alarm panel, and handed in their notice. Both ring the security desk. The SOC answers both from the same console.

Purview answers "which rule, which content, is the policy right?". Defender answers "is this part of a bigger attack, and what do I do to the user and device?".

## What actually matters

**1. The DLP alert lifecycle.** Six steps: **Trigger → Notify → Triage → Investigate → Remediate → Tune**. An alert fires only if the DLP policy is set to create one. It lands in two places: the Purview **DLP Alerts dashboard** and the Defender **incident queue**. ==!An alert does not prove bad intent.== Your job is to decide true positive, false positive, or "the policy needs tuning".

**2. 2026 change: DLP alerts may not reach the Defender queue.** A **built-in alert tuning rule for DLP signals** took effect in early **October 2026**. With it on, Defender XDR records DLP signals as **behaviors**, not alerts. They do not appear in the incident queue. They stay huntable in `BehaviorInfo` and `BehaviorEntities`. To get DLP alerts back in the queue, disable the rule at **Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR > Alert tuning**. Exam questions written before this assume DLP alerts are always in the queue. Answer those as asked; in the real portal, check the rule first.

**3. Investigating a DLP alert.**

| Where | What you see | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| **Purview** DLP > Alerts | **Details** (policy, rule, severity, workload), **Events** (the matched actions, file, policy-tip override), **User activity summary** (120 days, needs IRM data sharing) | Set status (e.g. Investigating, Resolved), assign, comment, **Copy event link** (read-only share), download / label / unshare / delete the file, send notification |
| **Defender** incident > alert | **Alert story**, **Related events**, **Sensitive info types** tab, **Source** tab (file content, if permitted), Copilot **Summarize** | Download email; apply sensitivity/retention label, unshare, delete; user card → reset password, disable; device card → isolate; **Go Hunt** built-in queries; **Manage incident** → classification |

Purview has **no classification field**. To close a false positive there, set **Resolved** and write the reason in a comment. Defender has the formal **True positive / False positive** classification. ==!Resolving does not suppress future alerts.== The next match fires again; fix the policy for that.

**4. Insider risk alerts.** An alert is built in five steps: **settings → policy → triggering event → activity scored → alert when the score passes the threshold**. New insights about the same user are **added to the existing alert**, not raised as a new one. On the alert page you get four views:

| Tab | Use it to |
|---|---|
| **All risk factors** | See *why the user is risky*: top exfiltration, cumulative exfiltration, **sequences**, priority content, unallowed domains, unusual behaviour. ==!These are not always what triggered the alert.== |
| **Activity explorer** | Event-level list with risk score per event; filter to this alert or all user activity |
| **User activity** | Bubble timeline / scatter plot; sequences shown as connected bubbles; 1, 3 or 6 months |
| **Data risk graph** | Visual map of the user's data interactions (retires 24 Nov 2026) |

Status values: **Needs review → Confirmed** (attached to a case) or **Dismissed** (benign); **Resolved** when its case closes. You can dismiss up to **400** alerts at once. Alerts left in **Needs review** are deleted after **120 days**. Active cases (limit **2,000**) are kept indefinitely; resolved cases are deleted **120 days** after resolution.

Two 2026 changes to the IRM alert screens. Purview is rolling out a **unified alert experience** (**Alerts (preview)**) that merges the classic dashboard and the Triage Agent view. In it, **Spotlight** (the old "look here first" badge, given to alerts with a risk score of **85+**) is removed from **31 August 2026**; use the **Needs attention** filter instead. The **Data risk graph** tab retires on **24 November 2026**. A question may still name both; know what they were.

**5. Insider risk cases.** A case is one **user**, one or more alerts, and a long-running investigation. Only **Insider Risk Management** or **Insider Risk Management Investigators** members can create one. Case tabs: Case overview, Alerts, User activity, Activity explorer, **Forensic evidence** (screen captures), **Content explorer** (copies of the files and mail), **Case notes**, **Contributors**. Case actions: **Send email notice** (does not close the case), **Escalate for investigation** (to an **eDiscovery (Premium)** case), **Run Power Automate flows**, **Create/view Teams team**. You close with **Resolve case** as **Benign** or **Confirmed policy violation**.

**6. Insider risk in the Defender portal.** It only works after a Purview admin turns on **Share user risk details with other security solutions** (Purview > Settings > Insider Risk Management > **Data sharing**). Then IRM alerts appear in the alert and incident queues (filter **Service source** = *Microsoft Purview Insider Risk Management*). Status and classification sync both ways within about **30 minutes**:

| Defender | Insider Risk Management |
|---|---|
| New, In progress | Needs review |
| Resolved + True positive | Confirmed |
| Resolved + Informational/expected activity or False positive | Dismissed |

Not carried over: email exfiltration events, risky AI usage events, third-party cloud app events, events before the alert, and excluded events.

**7. Who can see what.**

| Need | Role |
|---|---|
| DLP alerts in Defender (DLP + security alerts) | **Manage Alerts** or **View-Only Manage Alerts** |
| DLP alerts in Defender (DLP only) | Information Protection Analyst, DLP Compliance Management, View-Only DLP Compliance Management |
| DLP Alerts dashboard in Purview | Role group: Compliance Administrator, Compliance Data Administrator, Security Administrator, Security Operator, Security Reader, or an Information Protection Admin / Analyst / Investigator group |
| Download matched files | **Data classification content viewer** |
| IRM alerts in Defender | Defender **Security Operator** or **Security Reader** **plus** an IRM role group |
| IRM data in advanced hunting | **Insider Risk Management Analysts** or **Investigators** |

Inside Purview, the IRM role groups split the work:

| IRM role group | Alerts and cases | Content explorer + forensic evidence | Policies and settings |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Insider Risk Management** | Yes | Yes | Yes (all-in-one group) |
| **Insider Risk Management Admins** | ==!No== | No | Yes |
| **Insider Risk Management Analysts** | Yes | ==!No== | No |
| **Insider Risk Management Investigators** | Yes | Yes | No |

IRM is privacy-first: usernames are **pseudonymized by default**, so alerts show an anonymised ID until an admin turns that off.

With IRM data sharing on, a DLP alert also shows the user's **Insider risk severity** (High, Medium, Low or None). That tells a DLP analyst whether this person is already on IRM's radar.

**8. Copilot and agents.** Both dashboards offer **Summarize with Copilot**. Purview also runs an **Alert Triage Agent** for DLP and one for IRM. Each sorts alerts into **Needs attention**, **Less urgent** and **Not categorized** and writes an agent summary. They only triage alerts from **active** policies, not policies in simulation mode. Know they exist; the details belong to D2.1.8.

## Where it lives

| Task | Portal | Path |
|---|---|---|
| DLP alerts (compliance view) | Purview `purview.microsoft.com` | **Data loss prevention > Alerts** |
| DLP / IRM incidents (security view) | Defender `security.microsoft.com` | **Incidents & alerts > Incidents** > Add filter > **Service/detection sources** |
| Insider risk alerts | Purview | **Insider Risk Management > Alerts** |
| Insider risk cases | Purview | **Insider Risk Management > Cases** |
| Turn on IRM → Defender sharing | Purview | **Settings > Insider Risk Management > Data sharing** |
| Turn on IRM Teams integration | Purview | **Settings > Insider Risk Management > Microsoft Teams** |
| DLP alert tuning rule | Defender | **Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR > Alert tuning** |
| Reports | Purview | **Insider Risk Management > Reports** (last 30 days per widget) |

Hunting tables in the Defender portal:

| Table | Holds |
|---|---|
| `CloudAppEvents` | Audit events for Exchange, SharePoint, OneDrive, devices — the DLP investigation table |
| `BehaviorInfo`, `BehaviorEntities` | DLP signals turned into behaviors by the October 2026 tuning rule |
| `AlertInfo`, `AlertEvidence` | IRM alerts alongside every other product's alerts |
| `DataSecurityBehaviors`, `DataSecurityEvents` | IRM behaviours and enriched policy-violating events |

```kql
// What has this user done that broke a Purview policy this week?
// (needs IRM data sharing ON; the table has ObjectName, not FileName)
DataSecurityEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(7d)
| where AccountUpn =~ "megan@contoso.com"
| summarize Events = count() by ActionType, ObjectName
| order by Events desc
```

`CloudAppEvents` lets you hunt back **30 days** of audit logs for a DLP investigation. The **Go Hunt** button on a DLP event offers ready-made queries such as *File shared with*, *File activities*, *Site activity* and *User DLP violations for last 30 days*.

Retention differs by portal. Defender keeps DLP incidents for **six months**. The Purview *Learn about investigating DLP alerts* article says the DLP Alerts dashboard keeps alerts for **30 days**; the training unit says it depends on your **audit log retention**. ==!The two sources disagree.== For the exam, "Defender = 6 months, Purview dashboard = 30 days" is the documented pair; in a real tenant, check your audit retention.

## Exam traps

- **"Is this DLP alert part of a wider attack?"** → ==+Defender portal incident==. **"Which rule matched and does the policy need tuning?"** → ==+Purview DLP Alerts dashboard==.
- ==!Resolving a DLP alert stops it firing again.== ==+Resolve only closes this one. Tune the policy to stop repeats.==
- ==!IRM alerts appear in Defender automatically.== ==+Only after "Share user risk details with other security solutions" is turned on in Purview.==
- ==!An Insider Risk Management Admin can investigate alerts.== ==+Admins configure policies only; Analysts, Investigators or the all-in-one Insider Risk Management group investigate.== Only Investigators (or the all-in-one group) see **Content explorer** and **forensic evidence**.
- ==!Sentinel automation on IRM incidents just works.== IRM incidents synced from Defender carry **no alert content**, so Sentinel automation can fail. Microsoft recommends the ==+Microsoft Purview Insider Risk Management data connector== for Sentinel.
- ==!Defender Security Operator is enough to see IRM alerts.== ==+You also need an IRM role group (Insider Risk Management, Analysts or Investigators).==
- Defender **True positive** maps to IRM **Confirmed**; **False positive** and **Informational, expected activity** both map to ==@Dismissed==.
- ==!An IRM analyst can create a case.== The **Analysts** role group can triage. ==+Creating a case needs Insider Risk Management or Insider Risk Management Investigators.==
- ==!Sending an email notice closes the case.== ==+Only Resolve case closes it (Benign or Confirmed policy violation).==
- **Escalate for investigation** goes to ==@eDiscovery (Premium)== — not to Defender, not to Sentinel.
- **Active case limit:** the training unit says **100**; the current IRM docs' limits table says ==@2,000 active cases==. The docs are newer; prefer 2,000 unless a question quotes the module.
- ==!DLP signals are always in the incident queue.== Since October 2026 a built-in tuning rule turns them into ==@behaviors== (`BehaviorInfo`) until you disable it.
- **User activity summary** on a DLP alert = **120 days**, needs IRM data sharing and the user in scope of an IRM policy.
