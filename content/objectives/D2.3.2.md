## Azure first

- **Microsoft 365 content** lives in a few big stores: **Exchange Online** mailboxes (mail, calendar, and Teams chat copies), **SharePoint** sites and **OneDrive** accounts (files), **Microsoft 365 Groups**, **Teams** and **Viva Engage**. eDiscovery searches these stores **in place** — it does not need a log pipeline or a workspace.
- **Microsoft Purview** (`purview.microsoft.com`) is the compliance portal. **eDiscovery** is one of its solutions, next to **Audit** (D2.3.1).
- **Role groups** in Purview are bundles of roles. eDiscovery access is *not* inherited from Global Administrator — you must be put in the **eDiscovery Manager** role group explicitly.
- Licences: the core eDiscovery search, hold and export is in Microsoft 365 **E3** and **E5**. Premium features (review sets, analytics) need **E5**-level licences. An Azure-only learner can't run this — use the screenshots.

New to these? Read the Foundations pages linked above.

## In one sentence

**Content search** is the part of **Microsoft Purview eDiscovery** that searches the *contents* of mailboxes, Teams chats, SharePoint/OneDrive files and groups with **keywords and conditions** (written in **KeyQL**, the Keyword Query Language), inside a **case**, shows **statistics** or a **sample**, and **exports** the matching items.

## Why it exists

Audit (D2.3.1) tells you *that* a user opened or sent something. It doesn't give you the thing itself. eDiscovery is the **search warrant for the filing cabinets**: it opens the actual email bodies, attachments, chats and documents and finds the ones that match.

A SOC uses it after a phishing campaign ("every mailbox that received a message from `invoices@evil.example` with an `.html` attachment"), after a data leak ("files containing *Project Falcon* shared by the Sales team last month"), or for an insider case. Legal teams use the same tool for litigation — which is why so much of the training is about cases, holds and exports.

Everything happens **inside a case**. The case is the locked evidence room; membership is the key.

## What actually matters

**1. The new eDiscovery experience — know what changed.** Microsoft retired the **classic** Content search, eDiscovery (Standard) and eDiscovery (Premium) experiences on **31 August 2025** (outside 21Vianet; one legacy reference article's banner says 26 May 2025 — the legacy-solutions overview says 31 August 2025 for all three). There is now one **eDiscovery** solution in the Purview portal. "Content search" survives as a **system-generated case named *Content Search*** that holds every content search in the org, open by default to members of the eDiscovery Manager and Administrator role groups. Other new terms: **Statistics** replace *collections*; tasks are now **processes** (tracked in the **Process manager**); advanced indexing runs automatically; exam questions written for the old UI may still say *Content search > New search*.

**2. Permissions — the most-tested part.**

| Role group / subgroup | Can do |
|---|---|
| ==@eDiscovery Manager== (subgroup) | Search, preview, export; create cases and manage **cases they created or are members of** only |
| ==@eDiscovery Administrator== (subgroup) | Everything a Manager can, plus **see all cases**, add themselves to any case, configure eDiscovery settings, **remove members** from a case |
| Compliance Administrator / Organization Management | Include Case Management, Compliance Search and Hold — but **not Export or Preview** |
| Reviewer | Review sets only |

Key rules:

- **Global Administrator and Compliance Administrator cannot open cases or search content** unless explicitly added to the eDiscovery Manager role group — that's the training's rule (unit 3). ==!The product permissions table is more nuanced:== the Compliance Administrator and Organization Management role groups *do* hold Compliance Search and Case Management, but **not Preview or Export**, and they still only see cases they're members of. The product doc wins. For the exam: to search, preview **and** export, add the person to **eDiscovery Manager**.
- You need at least one eDiscovery role to even *see* the Cases list.
- Separate roles do separate jobs: **Compliance Search** (run searches, see estimates), **Preview** (see items), **Export** (download), **Hold**, **Case Management**, **RMS Decrypt** (read encrypted mail), **Search And Purge** (delete matching mail — only Organization Management has it by default).
- Assign in **Settings** > **Role groups** > **eDiscovery Manager** (the training unit shows it as **Settings** > **Roles and scopes** > **Role groups**) (needs Organization Management or the Role Management role). A mail-enabled security group can join the Manager subgroup, not the Administrator subgroup.

**3. Cases.** Every search belongs to a case. The creator is added as a member automatically; others must be added. Two ways in: **Create case**, then **Searches** tab > **Create search**; or the split button **Create case** ▾ > **Create search** (makes case and search in one go). Search names must be **unique in the organisation**.

**4. Data sources.** **Add sources** (people, groups, sites — enter a name, email or full SharePoint/OneDrive URL) or **Add tenant-wide sources** (*All people and groups*, with options for unlicensed/on-premises, guest, departed users and shared Teams channels). **Exclude inactive users** narrows scope. Narrow sources = faster, more relevant results.

**5. Building the query.** On the **Query** tab:

- **Condition builder.** The **Keywords** condition is always first and only uses **Equal**. Then **Add conditions**: *Date*, *Subject/Title*, *Participants*, *Sender/Author*, *Size*, *Message kind* (`email`, `im`, `microsoftteams`, `meetings`…), *Received*, *Sent*, *File type*, and more. Different conditions are joined by **AND** (the one after Keywords must be AND). Several values inside one condition are joined by **OR**.
- **KeyQL editor** (*Add conditions* > *KeyQL*). Paste or type a full query. It autocompletes properties and operators (after **two** characters), suggests values, and flags **Potential errors**. KeyQL and Keyword can each be used **once**, and a KeyQL condition stands alone.
- **Copilot** can turn a plain-English prompt into KeyQL (preview, needs Security Copilot). **Search by file** (preview) matches against an uploaded `.txt` or `.csv` up to **10 MB**.

**6. KeyQL rules worth memorising.**

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Operators belong in the **KeyQL** condition | The search-query article says the **Keywords** box treats AND/OR/NOT as literal words; the condition-builder article says AND/OR/NEAR work there. Sources disagree — use the KeyQL editor and it works either way |
| `property:value`, no space after the colon | `from:pilarp@contoso.com` (`to: pilarp` becomes a plain keyword) |
| Operators **AND**, **OR**, **NOT**, **NEAR** must be **UPPERCASE** | `budget AND forecast` |
| A **space** between terms means **OR** | `from:"Sara Davis" subject:reorg` |
| Quotes for multi-word values | `subject:"budget Q1"` |
| **Prefix** wildcard only | `subject:set*` works; `*set` does not |
| Minus excludes | `-from:"Sara Davis"` |
| Ranges and dates | `sent>=2026-01-01`, `size:1..1000` |
| Searches are **case-insensitive** and times are **UTC** | `cat` = `CAT` |
| An **empty** query returns **everything** in the sources | — |

Message headers are not indexed, so you can't search them. You also can't search for an empty property (`subject:""` returns nothing).

**7. Run, review, export.** **Run query** and choose **Statistics** (counts, size, top locations, per-keyword hits if you entered two or more keyword lines) or **Sample** (a random sample of real items: 1, 10 or 100 items per location, from 10 to 10,000 locations). Then **Export**:

- choose **indexed items that match**, **indexed + partially indexed items**, or **partially indexed only**;
- options for SharePoint/OneDrive **versions**, Teams/Exchange **threaded conversations** (HTML transcripts), **contextual chat** messages, **cloud attachments**;
- format **PST** or **MSG**, organised by source, with or without folder paths;
- **Export items report only** (CSV metadata, no items) or items with report;
- track in **Process manager**, download from the case's **Exports** tab (Export role needed). ==!Search export packages are deleted after 14 days.==

**Holds** (preserve content) are in the core feature set. **Premium** licences add **review sets** (copy results for tagging, analytics, predictive coding). The overview's feature table also lists cloud attachments and SharePoint versions as premium, although the export dialog shows the options.

**8. Search and purge — the SOC's phishing clean-up.** Content search finds the mail; **Security & Compliance PowerShell** deletes it (Microsoft Graph can also purge on premium cases). The eDiscovery search page has no purge button for standard cases.

| Step | What |
|---|---|
| Connect | `Connect-IPPSSession -EnableSearchOnlySession` (ExchangeOnlineManagement 3.9.0 or later) |
| Search | `New-ComplianceSearch -Name "Remove Phishing Message" -ExchangeLocation All -ContentMatchQuery '...'`, then `Start-ComplianceSearch` |
| Delete | `New-ComplianceSearchAction -SearchName "Remove Phishing Message" -Purge -PurgeType SoftDelete` (or `HardDelete`) |
| Status | `Get-ComplianceSearchAction`; the action is named `SearchName_Purge` (your search name + `_Purge`) |
| Roles | search = **eDiscovery Manager** or **Compliance Search**; delete = **Search And Purge** (in **Organization Management** of the *Purview* portal — Exchange's Organization Management doesn't count) |
| Limits | **10 items per mailbox** per run (100 via Graph on premium cases); max **50,000 mailboxes** in the search; **mailboxes and public folders only**, no SharePoint/OneDrive; Teams items and unindexed items aren't deleted |

SoftDelete moves mail to the user's Recoverable Items folder (user can recover it). HardDelete marks it for permanent removal. A hold still preserves purged mail until the hold ends. If you have **Defender for Office 365 Plan 2**, Microsoft says use its email remediation instead (Remediate malicious email delivered in Office 365).

## Where it lives

All in the **Microsoft Purview portal** (`purview.microsoft.com`).

| Task | Path |
|---|---|
| Cases list | **Solutions** > **eDiscovery** > **Cases** |
| Content Search case | **eDiscovery** > **Content Search** |
| New search | case > **Searches** tab > **Create search** (or **Create case** ▾ > **Create search**) |
| Build query | search > **Query** tab > **Data sources** + **Condition builder** / **KeyQL** > **Run query** |
| Export | search > **Export**; download from case > **Exports** tab |
| Content Search case (first time) | **eDiscovery** > **Content Search** > **Create case** |
| Permissions | **Settings** > **Role groups** > **eDiscovery Manager** (training: Settings > Roles and scopes > Role groups) |

PowerShell equivalents live in **Security & Compliance PowerShell** (`Connect-IPPSSession`): `New-ComplianceSearch`, `Start-ComplianceSearch`, `New-ComplianceSearchAction -Purge`, `Add-RoleGroupMember "eDiscoveryManager"`, `Add-eDiscoveryCaseAdmin` (needs the Case Management role first).

```text
from:invoices@evil.example AND hasattachment:true AND sent>=2026-10-01
(subject:"password reset" OR subject:"mailbox full") AND kind:email
"Project Falcon" AND filetype:docx -author:"Ann Beebe"
```

## Exam traps

- ==!KeyQL is KQL, so use `| where`.== ==+eDiscovery uses the Keyword Query Language: `property:value` with AND/OR/NOT/NEAR.== Kusto (KQL) is for Sentinel and advanced hunting.
- ==!A Global Administrator can open any eDiscovery case.== ==+Only members of the eDiscovery Manager role group — and for other people's cases, only eDiscovery Administrators or case members.==
- ==!An eDiscovery Manager can see every case.== ==+Only cases they're a member of.== Seeing all cases = **eDiscovery Administrator**.
- ==!Compliance Administrator can export results.== The **Export** and **Preview** roles sit in the eDiscovery Manager role group, not Compliance Administrator.
- ==!Leaving the keyword box empty returns nothing.== ==+It returns everything in the selected sources.==
- ==!Lowercase `and` works as an operator.== Operators must be **uppercase**; lowercase is searched as a word.
- ==!`*invoice` finds "fakeinvoice".== Only **prefix** wildcards (`invoice*`).
- ==!Content search is a separate tool from eDiscovery.== Not any more — it's the *Content Search* case inside the unified eDiscovery experience.
- Need to **delete** a phishing mail from many mailboxes → ==+`New-ComplianceSearchAction -Purge`== with the **Search And Purge** role (Organization Management by default). ==!eDiscovery Manager alone can't purge.==
- ==!Purge removes every match.== ==+Max 10 items per mailbox per run, Exchange only, 50,000 mailboxes max.== ==!It cleans SharePoint/OneDrive too.== It doesn't.
- ==!Search export packages stay forever.== ==+Deleted after 14 days.==
- "Search finds the attacker's *activity*" → that's **Audit** (D2.3.1). eDiscovery finds the **content**.

## Skip

- **Skip:** the training's legal storyline (litigation, data subject requests). Know that eDiscovery serves SOC investigations too.
- **Skip:** review sets, tagging, predictive coding, analytics, custodians and legal-hold notifications. They are eDiscovery **Premium** legal workflow, not SOC content search.
- **Skip:** app-only (certificate) authentication for eDiscovery PowerShell. It is unsupported and admin plumbing.
- **Skip:** the full condition and property tables (Item class, Retention label, Sensitive information type, Topic…). Know Date, Subject/Title, Sender/Participants, Message kind, File type.
- **Skip:** `c:c` / `c:s` notation in generated queries. Never type it yourself; that's all you need.
- **Skip:** the 21Vianet-only classic Content search articles.
