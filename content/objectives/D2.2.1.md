## Azure first

- **Defender for Endpoint (MDE)** is Microsoft's EDR product. A sensor on each laptop or server (the process `MsSense.exe`) streams what happens on that machine to the cloud.
- An **onboarded device** is a machine running that sensor. It appears in the **device inventory** of the **Microsoft Defender portal** (`security.microsoft.com`). Every investigation in this lesson starts from that list.
- **EDR** (endpoint detection and response) means "record everything, so an analyst can replay it". The timeline is the replay screen. EDR is a **Defender for Endpoint Plan 2** capability. Plan 1 does not include it.
- **MITRE ATT&CK** is the public catalogue of attacker techniques (T1059 = Command and Scripting Interpreter, and so on). The timeline tags events with these IDs.
- An Azure-subscription-only learner *can* see a real timeline: turning on **Defender for Servers** (Defender for Cloud) for an Azure VM onboards it to MDE, and it then shows up in the Defender portal device inventory.

New to these? Read the Foundations pages linked above.

## In one sentence

The **device timeline** is the **Timeline** tab of a device's page in the Defender portal: a searchable, filterable, exportable list of every process, file, network, registry, logon and alert event the sensor saw on that one machine, in time order.

## Why it exists

An alert tells you *one* bad thing happened. It does not tell you what came before it or after it. Think of CCTV in a shop. The alarm (the alert) says "a window broke at 02:14". The CCTV recording (the timeline) shows the person arriving at 02:09, testing the door, breaking the window, walking to the till and leaving at 02:20.

You scroll back to find **initial access** (how they got in). You scroll forward to find **persistence** (what they left behind) and **lateral movement** (where they went next). The timeline answers "what is the full story on this machine?" before you take action on it in D2.2.2.

## What actually matters

**1. Getting to the device page.** Microsoft lists several doors: **Assets > Devices** (the inventory), an alert in the alerts queue, a device in an incident, the global search box (choose **Device**), a file page, or an IP / domain page. All lead to the same device page. The **Timeline** tab is one tab of that page.

**2. The device inventory columns you must recognise.**

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ==@Risk level== | Driven by **active alerts** (type and severity). Resolving alerts lowers it. |
| ==@Exposure level== | Driven by **pending security recommendations** (vulnerabilities, missing patches). Low / Medium / High. "No data available" if the device has not reported for over 30 days. |
| Health state | **Active**, **Inactive** (no signal for more than **seven days**), **Misconfigured** (No sensor data / Impaired communications). |
| Antivirus status | Disabled, Not reporting, Not updated. |

Risk and exposure are a classic confusable. Risk = alerts. Exposure = recommendations.

**3. The rest of the device page.** Tabs: **Overview**, **Incidents and alerts**, **Timeline**, **Configuration management** (Security policies, Effective settings), **Security recommendations**, **Inventories** (software, vulnerable components, browser extensions, certificates), **Discovered vulnerabilities**, **Missing KBs**, **Security baselines**, and **Sentinel events** if Sentinel is onboarded to the Defender portal. Overview cards: **Active alerts**, **Logged on users** (past **30 days**), **Security assessments**, **Device health status**. The sources disagree on the Active alerts card window: training unit 3 says the last **30 days**; the current device entity page article says the last **six months**. The product article is newer, so trust it. The response-action bar across the top is lesson D2.2.2.

**4. Timeline defaults and limits.**

| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Default range | Past **30 days** |
| Quick ranges | Past day, week, 30 days, or a **custom** range |
| Jump in time | Drag/highlight a section of the time bar. Arrows on the bar mark **automated investigations**. |
| Export | CSV for the current day or a range of **up to seven days** |
| Retention | Sources disagree. The device-investigation article says **90 days** by default. The data storage and privacy article says Defender for Endpoint data is kept **180 days**, visible across the portal. The privacy article is the one that defines retention, so trust **180 days**. Either way, a connected Sentinel / Log Analytics workspace can keep a copy longer. |
| Advanced hunting reach | **30 days**. *Hunt for related events* runs there, so it cannot reach older timeline events. |
| Firewall events | Shown only if the Windows audit policy **Audit Filtering Platform Connection** is on. Events **5025** (firewall service stopped), **5031** (app blocked from accepting inbound connections), **5157** (connection blocked). |

**5. Reading one event.** Select a row. A side panel opens with event details and, where data exists, a small **graph of related entities** (parent process, child process, file). Two buttons matter:

- **Hunt for related events** opens **advanced hunting** (the KQL query screen) with a pre-built query: this event plus other events around the same time on the same device.
- The **process tree** shows parent-child chains (for example `winword.exe` → `powershell.exe`).

The **Additional information** column adds labels such as *Active threat detected*, *Remediation successful*, *Remediation unsuccessful*, *Warning bypassed by user* (SmartScreen ignored), *Suspicious script detected*, *Contained by Application Guard*, and the **alert category** (for example *Lateral Movement*) when the event raised an alert.

**6. MITRE ATT&CK techniques.** **Techniques** are a second **data type** in the timeline, next to normal events. They appear in **bold** with a **blue icon**. The technique ID and name show as tags under Additional information. Select one for a side pane with tactic and description. You can copy entity details (a file's SHA1, a command line) from the blue copy icon. **Hunt for related events** from a technique returns the related events but ==!not the technique row itself==.

**7. Flags.** Select the **flag icon** in the **Flag** column to mark an event. Use flags to highlight the important events, mark ones that need a deep dive, and build a **clean breach timeline**. To see only them: **Filters** > turn on **Flagged events** > **Apply**. Clicking the time bar after that narrows to events *before* the flagged event.

**8. Filters and columns.** **Filters** lets you choose the **Data type** (events or techniques) and the **Event group** (for example *ASR events*). **Customize columns** chooses which columns show. Search finds a specific event by text.

**9. Unified timeline (Preview).** If Microsoft Sentinel is onboarded to the Defender portal, device activities from Sentinel's **Sentinel events** tab also appear on the main Timeline tab (as of January 2025). To turn that off: **Timeline settings** > **Stream events from Microsoft Sentinel queries** = **Off**. The **Sentinel events** tab itself shows Sentinel alerts, hunting **bookmarks**, **anomalies** and **activities**, plus **insights** (pre-built queries).

**10. Two odd timeline events.** If the sensor (`MsSense.exe`) runs low on resources it enters **Critical** mode, stops processing new events, and writes an event saying so on the timeline. It returns to normal by itself. Also, the **Last seen** value on the device page can be older than the newest timeline event. Trust the timeline.

**11. Internet-facing devices.** Windows devices reachable from the internet get an **internet facing** tag, from Microsoft external scans or observed inbound external connections. The tag drops off after **48 hours** with no new events. Hunting: `DeviceInfo` column `IsInternetFacing`.

## Where it lives

All of this is in the **Microsoft Defender portal** (`security.microsoft.com`). There is no Azure-portal or Purview equivalent.

| Task | Path |
|---|---|
| Device inventory | **Assets** > **Devices** (older docs: **Endpoints** > **Device inventory**) |
| Open a timeline | device page > **Timeline** tab |
| Device from an incident | Legacy page: **Investigation & response** > **Incidents & alerts** > **Incidents** > incident > **Assets** > **Devices**. New incident cases path: see D2.2.3 *Where it lives*. |
| Flag view | Timeline > **Filters** > **Flagged events** > **Apply** |
| Sentinel stream toggle | Timeline > **Timeline settings** > **Stream events from Microsoft Sentinel queries** |
| Export | Timeline > **Export** (up to 7 days) |

Hunting equivalent of "internet-facing devices":

```kql
DeviceInfo
| where Timestamp > ago(7d)
| where IsInternetFacing
| summarize arg_max(Timestamp, *) by DeviceId
```

## Exam traps

- ==!Risk level comes from vulnerabilities.== No. ==+Risk level = active alerts; exposure level = pending security recommendations.==
- ==!You can export 30 days of timeline at once.== ==+Export is capped at seven days per export==, even though the default *view* is 30 days.
- ==!Flagging an event changes the alert or incident.== ==+A flag is an analyst bookmark on the timeline only.== Use the **Flagged events** filter to see them.
- ==!Hunt for related events from a technique returns the technique too.== It returns the related events but not the technique row.
- ==!Firewall blocks always appear on the timeline.== ==+Only when the Audit Filtering Platform Connection audit policy is on== (events 5025, 5031, 5157).
- ==!The timeline is in the Azure portal / Sentinel.== ==+It is the device page in the Defender portal.== Sentinel adds a *Sentinel events* tab only when onboarded to the Defender portal.
- **Inactive** = no signal for **more than 7 days**. Not 30. (30 days is when exposure stops being computed.)
- "Device is busy and the newest events are missing" → the sensor went into **Critical** mode. It recovers on its own.
- ==!Plan 1 has a device timeline.== ==+The timeline is EDR, a Defender for Endpoint Plan 2 capability.== Plan 1 has no EDR.
- ==!Timeline data and advanced hunting keep the same history.== ==+The portal keeps 180 days; advanced hunting queries reach 30 days.==

## Skip

- **Skip:** unit 4 *Use behavioral blocking* and unit 5 *Detect devices with device discovery* of *Perform device investigations in Microsoft Defender for Endpoint*. Both are mapped off-blueprint. Know only that EDR in block mode exists. The exam does not ask about discovery modes here.
- **Skip:** the Security recommendations, Software inventory, Discovered vulnerabilities and Missing KBs tabs beyond their names. They are vulnerability management, not timeline investigation.
- **Skip:** the long advanced-hunting query in the internet-facing section (`extractjson` of every field). Know the `IsInternetFacing` column and the 48-hour tag rule.
- **Skip:** the device health status message priority list (13 messages). Know that the card shows Defender Antivirus mode and scan/update status.
- **Skip:** *Report device inaccuracy* steps, *View in map*, *Set criticality* and *Device value*. These are Exposure Management features, not timeline skills.
