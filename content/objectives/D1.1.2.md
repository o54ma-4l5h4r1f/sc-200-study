## Azure first

- An **alert** is one suspicious signal ("this file looks malicious on this laptop"). An **incident** is the case file that groups related alerts into one attack story. The Defender portal builds incidents for you.
- A **false positive** is an alert about something that is actually fine — a known admin tool, a security test. Too many of them bury the real attacks.
- A **device group** is a named set of devices in Defender for Endpoint, used to limit which analysts see which machines.
- **Microsoft Sentinel** (the SIEM — the log store and rule engine) now lives inside the Defender portal too. Its **analytics rules** create alerts that join the same incident queue.
- **Licence:** needs Defender XDR / Defender for Endpoint in a Microsoft 365 tenant. With an Azure subscription only, the screenshots below are the lab.

New to these? Read the Foundations pages linked above.

## In one sentence

This bullet is three dials on the alert pipeline: **notifications** (who gets emailed when an endpoint alert fires), **tuning** (rules that hide, resolve or downgrade alerts you know are harmless — formerly *suppression*), and **correlation** (how alerts are glued into incidents, and how to opt rules out).

## Why it exists

Think of a building's fire alarm system. **Notifications** decide whose pager rings. **Tuning** is telling the system "the kitchen toaster sets off detector 4 every morning — ignore detector 4 when the toaster is on". **Correlation** is the control panel noticing that detectors 4, 5 and 9 all fired within a minute and opening **one** fire case, not three.

A SOC drowning in duplicate, harmless alerts misses the real one. These three settings are how you turn the noise down without turning the protection off.

## What actually matters

### 1. Alert notifications (Defender for Endpoint)

- Path: **Settings > Endpoints > General > Email notifications**, **Alerts** tab, **Add item**.
- General settings: **Rule name**, **Include organization name**, **Include tenant-specific portal link**, **Include device information** (puts the device name in the email body), **Devices**, **Alert severity**.
- **Devices** = all devices *or* selected device groups. ==!All devices is Global Administrator only.==
- Then add recipients (**Add recipient**), **Send test email**, **Save notification rule**.
- Permission: **Manage security settings**. With basic permissions, **Security Administrator** or **Global Administrator**. With RBAC, users manage rules only inside their device-group scope, and recipients only get alerts for the device groups in the rule. Only Global Administrator manages rules for **all device groups**.
- The email holds basic alert facts and a link to the portal. New recipients only get alerts raised after they are added.

### 2. Alert tuning (the new name for alert suppression)

- An **alert tuning rule** = conditions + an action. Conditions are built on **evidence types** (Microsoft calls them IOCs, indicators of compromise): files, processes, scheduled tasks, AMSI scripts, WMI events and more. ==@Up to 200 conditions== per rule, joined with **AND / OR** and groups. Condition values are not case sensitive; some properties take wildcards.
- **Three actions:**

| Action | What happens | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Hide alert** | Alert suppressed, **no incident created**. Still in the `AlertInfo` and `AlertEvidence` tables for hunting | ==!Defender for Endpoint alerts only== |
| **Resolve alert** | Alert and its incidents created already in **Resolved** status | — |
| **Set as behavior** | Turned into a *behavior*: not in the alert queue, no incident. Data stays in `BehaviorInfo` and `BehaviorEntities` | Not for Defender for Cloud or Defender for Office 365 alerts |

- **Scope by source.** You pick **service sources** and (preview) **detection sources**. Picking a whole service source also covers its **custom detections**.
- **Built-in rules.** Microsoft ships built-in tuning rules for common benign activity. They don't stop AIR (automated investigation) or email notifications. If AIR finds something malicious, the alert is **reactivated**. They ==!do not apply to custom detections or Custom TI (indicators)==. You can **Disable** one.
- **Two ways in:** from **Settings** (**Add new rule**) or from an alert (**Tune alert** on the alert page) — the second pre-fills the conditions.
- **Editing a rule** offers to **release** alerts it already suppressed.
- **After tuning from an alert**, the success page offers to add the alert's IOCs (files etc.) to an **allow list** as indicators, scoped to the alert's scope. That is a separate, optional step: tuning hides the *alert*; the allow indicator stops the *block* (see D1.1.4).
- Use it for known internal tools and planned security tests, not to silence alerts you haven't understood.

### 3. Correlation

- The correlation engine puts each new alert into a **new** incident (if unique) or an **existing** one (if related within a time frame). Its logic is proprietary — you cannot edit it.
- It keeps going: related **incidents are merged**. The contents of the *source* incident move into the *target*; the source is **closed** and gets a `Redirected` tag. No new incident is created. You do not choose the direction. The engine merges on shared **entities** (users, devices, mailboxes), **artifacts** (files, processes, email senders), **time frames** and **sequences of events** (a phishing email followed by a click).
- **No automatic merge if:** one incident is **Closed**; they are **assigned to different people**; they have **different classifications or determinations**; the target would exceed the entity maximum; or they contain devices in **different device groups** (only when that option is enabled).
- **Manual fixes:** move an alert to another incident, or **merge manually — up to five incidents at a time** (preferred, because the activity log is kept).
- **Recommended correlations** (attack story filter, plus a Summary-tab card) shows related incidents that were *not* merged. It ==!changes nothing== — no grouping, no merge; those incidents keep their own owner and status.
- **Opt rules out.** **Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR > Rules > Incident correlation** holds the **Incident correlation default setting**. It is ==@off by default== — Sentinel analytics rules are **excluded** from correlation until you change it. Per rule: **Tenant default / Enabled / Disabled** in the rule wizard, which writes `#INC_CORR#` or `#DONT_CORR#` at the start of the rule description. Managing this for analytics rules needs **Microsoft Sentinel Contributor**. **Custom detections** are **included** by default and set per rule (preview) — there is ==!no tenant-level setting== for them. Excluded alerts group only by the analytics rule's own grouping settings, exactly as in classic Sentinel. Any matching **incident alert-grouping rule** stays the final authority.
- **Sentinel workspaces:** only the **primary** workspace's alerts correlate with Defender alerts. **Secondary** workspaces never correlate.
- **Restrict correlation to within scoped device groups** is a Defender for Endpoint **advanced feature** (see D1.1.3). It splits cross-group incidents so a local SOC can act on them. It only affects future correlation.

### 4. Alert service settings

**Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR > Alert service settings** chooses which alerts a service sends. For **Microsoft Entra ID Protection** the new default is **High-risk detections only**; options are **High + Medium** or **All detections**.

## Where it lives

All in the **Microsoft Defender portal** (`security.microsoft.com`).

| Task | Path |
|---|---|
| Endpoint alert emails | **Settings > Endpoints > General > Email notifications > Alerts** |
| Alert tuning rules (current) | **System > Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR > Rules > Alert tuning** (`security.microsoft.com/securitysettings/defender/alert_suppression`) |
| Legacy endpoint suppression list | **Settings > Endpoints > Rules > Alert suppression** |
| Tune from one alert | **Incidents & alerts > Alerts** > open alert > **Tune alert** |
| Correlation default | **Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR > Rules > Incident correlation** |
| Alert service settings | **Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR > Alert service settings** |
| Scoped-group correlation | **Settings > Endpoints > Advanced features** |

> **Two names, one feature:** training unit 4 calls it **Alert tuning** under **Settings > Microsoft Defender XDR**. The Defender for Endpoint doc *Manage suppression rules* still says **Settings > Endpoints > Rules > Alert suppression** and shows rule **status, scope, action, number of matching alerts, created by** and date. Microsoft's alert article says *"Alert tuning (previously alert suppression)"*. Learn **alert tuning** as the current name; expect either word in a question.

## Exam traps

- "Stop a known admin script from creating **incidents**, but keep the data for hunting" → ==+Hide alert== (endpoint alerts) or ==+Set as behavior==. ==!Not an indicator==, not an antivirus exclusion — those change *protection*, tuning changes *alerting*.
- "Close matching alerts automatically but keep them visible" → ==+Resolve alert==.
- **Hide alert** on a Defender for Office 365 alert → ==!not supported== (Hide is endpoint-only).
- Built-in tuning rules covering **custom detections** → ==!false==.
- Sentinel analytics-rule alerts are **not** correlated into Defender incidents by default → true; ==+turn on the Incident correlation default setting or tag the rule `#INC_CORR#`==.
- Two incidents assigned to **different analysts** will ==!not auto-merge==. Unassign one, then merge manually.
- "Email the regional team only about **their** laptops' High alerts" → ==+Endpoints email notification rule scoped to their device group, severity High==. ==!All devices requires Global Administrator.==
- Changing correlation settings ==!does not re-correlate old alerts== — only future ones.
