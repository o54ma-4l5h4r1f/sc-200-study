# D1 — how it all connects

Domain 1 is about **setting up** the security operations environment. The objective pages teach each setting on its own. These seven pictures show how the settings join up. Read them after a few objective pages, then come back before the exam.

Dashed, muted boxes are **forward-links**: a construct from Domain 2 or Domain 3 that looks like a D1 thing or sits next to it. They are pointers only. The detail lives in that domain.

## 1 · The pipeline — from a log line to a playbook

Every D1 objective is one stage of this pipeline. Logs come **in** (top row), get turned into **alerts and incidents**, and then **automation** acts on them (bottom row). If you can place an objective on this picture, you know what it is for. The table a log lands in decides which rules can see it, which is why stage 4 matters so much.

## 2 · Four things that "act automatically" — and how they differ

Four D1 features all sound like "automation". They are not interchangeable. The left two are **Sentinel** features where *you* write the logic. The right two are **Defender XDR** engines where *Microsoft* writes the logic and you only set the limits. The commonest trap: an automation rule cannot email anyone or block anything. Only a playbook reaches other systems.

Objectives summarised: [D1.1.10](../objective/D1.1.10.html) · [D1.1.11](../objective/D1.1.11.html) · [D1.1.7](../objective/D1.1.7.html) · [D1.1.8](../objective/D1.1.8.html). Each page has its own flow diagram; this one only lines them up.

## 3 · The detection family — who writes the logic

There are many ways to turn table rows into alerts. They differ in **who writes the logic** and **where the rule lives**. Sentinel analytics rules come in seven types. Custom detections are the Defender XDR way, and Microsoft now recommends them for new rules. Anomaly rules are the odd one out: they never raise an alert.

## 7 · One dial, three readers — the device-group automation level

Every Defender for Endpoint device lands in exactly **one device group**. Each group carries an **automation level**. That single setting is read by AIR *and* by attack disruption, and they read it differently. Email AIR ignores it completely. How groups are matched and who may see them is drawn on [D1.1.9](../objective/D1.1.9.html); this picture is only about what the level changes.
