# D2 — how incident response fits together

Domain 2 is about **responding**. Something has already fired. The objective pages teach one product or one tool at a time. These seven pictures show how they join up into one SOC workflow. Read them after a few objective pages, then come back before the exam.

Dashed, muted boxes are **forward-links**: a construct from Domain 1 or Domain 3 that sits next to a D2 thing. They are pointers only. The detail lives in that domain.

## 1 · The loop — from a signal to a closed incident

Every D2 objective is a step on this loop. Products raise **alerts**, correlation joins them into an **incident**, and in the Defender portal an **incident case** wraps that incident. Then the analyst triages, investigates, remediates and closes. Closing forces a **classification**, and the two portals word the same verdicts differently. That mapping is a favourite exam question.

## 2 · Which product saw it — and what each one can do

Eight products raise the alerts in Domain 2. Each sees one kind of signal, has one main investigation screen, and owns its own response verbs. All eight feed the **Defender portal incident queue**, where correlation joins them. Three have a second home: Entra ID Protection in the Entra admin center, Purview in the Purview portal, and Defender for Cloud in the Azure portal. Mind the exceptions under the picture: some alerts never reach the queue.

## 3 · One attack, five products, one incident

A real intrusion crosses domains. The phish lands in email, the malware runs on a laptop, the stolen credential moves through Active Directory, and the data leaves through a cloud app. Each product raises its **own** alert for its own stage. The **correlation engine** joins them into one incident, and you read it as one **attack story**. Then you contain everything at once.

Objectives summarised: [D2.1.9](../objective/D2.1.9.html) · [D2.1.1](../objective/D2.1.1.html) · [D2.2.1](../objective/D2.2.1.html) · [D2.1.6](../objective/D2.1.6.html) · [D2.1.4](../objective/D2.1.4.html) · [D2.1.2](../objective/D2.1.2.html). ## 6 · Three Microsoft 365 logs, three questions

After an account takeover, three tools answer three different questions. **Purview Audit** says what the account *did*. **eDiscovery Content search** finds the actual *content*: mail bodies, chats, files. **Graph activity logs** show every API *call* the attacker's token made. Entra sign-in logs only tell you the token was issued. Pick the tool by the question, and don't confuse KeyQL with KQL.

Objectives summarised: [D2.3.1](../objective/D2.3.1.html) · [D2.3.2](../objective/D2.3.2.html) · [D2.3.3](../objective/D2.3.3.html). Each page has its own flow diagram; this one puts them side by side.

## 7 · Look-alikes — many buttons on one user, three kinds of "case"

The same compromised user can be acted on from four places. The buttons sound alike but do different things. Three of them **raise the user's Entra ID risk**, which hands the job to a risk-based Conditional Access policy. **Contain user** is the odd one out: it never touches the account. Below that, three different products each have something called a **case**.
